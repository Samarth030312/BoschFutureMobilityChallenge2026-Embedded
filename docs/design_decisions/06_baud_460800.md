# Decision 06 — 460 800 baud on USART2

## What the source confirms

- `huart2.Init.BaudRate = 460800` (`usart.c:75`).
- 8N1 (`usart.c:76-78`), oversampling 16 (`usart.c:81`), no HW flow control (`usart.c:80`).

## Why 460 800 specifically

**Throughput budget:**

Publish path: `/microros/imu_raw` at 100 Hz × ~280 bytes XRCE-encoded ≈ 28 000 B/s = **224 kbps** raw data.

Subscribe path: `/control/command` at ~20 Hz × ~30 bytes ≈ 600 B/s = **4.8 kbps**.

Total: ~230 kbps of payload. At 460 800 baud, 10 bits per byte (8 data + start + stop) → 46 080 bytes/s = ~368 kbps capacity. Used: ~50 %. **Comfortable headroom for XRCE session control + retransmits.**

**BRR generation accuracy:**

At 84 MHz APB1 clock and oversampling 16, BRR = `84000000 / (16 × 460800) = 11.39` → BRR = 11 with fractional 6 → effective baud = `84000000 / (16 × 11.375) = 461 538`. Error: ~0.16 % nominal.

At HSI ±1 % , worst-case rate is `460 800 × 1.0116 = 466 100` or `460 800 × 0.9884 = 455 500`. Total worst-case error: ~1.3 %. UART tolerates ~4-5 % per bit; safe.

## Why not higher (921 600, 1 Mbps)

Reasonable in principle, but:
- BRR at 921 600 from 84 MHz: `84e6 / (16 × 921600) = 5.696` → BRR = 5 + fractional → coarser resolution; HSI drift becomes more critical.
- At 2 Mbps the BRR resolution is too coarse for HSI accuracy.
- 460 800 leaves headroom for the team's needs without pushing the link.

## Why not lower (115 200)

- 115 200 baud × 0.8 (margin) = ~92 000 bps usable. Publish path alone is 224 kbps — wouldn't fit.

## Engineering math (for the audit trail)

USART BRR computation at 84 MHz, oversampling 16:

| Baud | BRR | Actual baud | Error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 115 200 | 45.57 | 115 234 | 0.03 % |
| 230 400 | 22.79 | 230 469 | 0.03 % |
| 460 800 | 11.39 | 461 538 | 0.16 % |
| 921 600 | 5.696 | 932 203 | 1.15 % |
| 1 Mbps | 5.25 | 1 058 824 | 5.88 % (too high) |

460 800 is the sweet spot for HSI + 84 MHz APB1.

## What the source comment confirms

`it_transport.c:27-28`: "2 KB ring — absorbs ~40 ms of sustained 460800-baud traffic. RAM budget is tight (97 % used) so larger isn't feasible right now."

The team did the bandwidth math.

## Drawbacks visible in current source

- **No tested margin for higher rates.** If a future feature needs more throughput (e.g., diagnostic publishers added), the link might need upgrading.
- **HSI clock drift over temperature**  eats into the baud-rate margin. An HSE crystal would help.

## Alternatives the choice rules out

- **921 600** — would require HSE or runtime HSI calibration; doesn't fit cleanly at 84 MHz APB1.
- **1 Mbps** — BRR error too high for HSI.
- **3 Mbps** (typical USB CDC native) — requires USB native; not the ST-LINK bridge limit.

## What a future redesign might pick

- **HSE + 921 600** for double the throughput margin.
- **USB CDC native** for ~12 Mbps. Adds USB device stack complexity.

## What this decision constrains in current code

- Buffer sizing assumes ~46 KB/s sustained.
- Worst-case publish time (`HAL_UART_Transmit` blocking ~2 ms per 80-byte XRCE message — see Decision 12) is calculated at 460 800.
- The team's "we lose ~1.9 ms per publish" reasoning depends on this baud rate.

Doubling the baud rate halves the per-publish blocking time — likely worth the change but requires bench verification.
